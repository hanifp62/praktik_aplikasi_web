Scope and method. This file covers the safety angle only. Offline-app mechanics (download units, storage, sync, paywalls) are in `01-aplikasi-outdoor-luring.md`. **All sources were accessed on 2026-09-25.** Verification tags: **[F]** means the page was read with a fetch tool (quotes pass through a summarising fetch, so small normalisations are possible). **[API]** means a Zendesk help article was read through the public Help Center API (`/api/v2/help_center/en-us/articles/{id}.json`, the same method as file 01), with the article's own `updated_at` date. **[S]** means the claim was seen only in a search-engine result summary because the page was blocked (403/429/CAPTCHA) or not opened; confidence is lower. **[PDF]** means text was extracted locally from the official PDF with `pdftotext`. Publication dates are given next to each item. Items dated before 2025 are marked "(older)".

## Q1. Incident evidence: where phone or app reliance contributed (UK, US, Canada, NZ, Indonesia)

### Takeaway
Rescue organisations in the UK, Canada and NZ keep naming the same contributors to call-outs: unsuitable or online-only mapping apps, phones that die mid-route, and people unable to use the technology. None of the national datasets found (MREW, Scottish Mountain Rescue, NZMSC, Basarnas) has a category that quantifies phone or app failure. The best numbers are indirect: "lost", "navigation error" and "human factors" categories, plus a 2022 Scottish survey in which more than 40% of respondents reported that a phone or GPS had stopped working. The specific mechanisms (an app leads someone onto a risky or unofficial route; the battery dies while navigating) are documented only in anecdotes. Those anecdotes are consistent across countries, including Indonesia in 2025–2026.

### Inferences
- The consistent causal story across the UK, Canada, the US and Indonesia (INFERENCE from convergent anecdotes and official statements, not from quantified data) has three layers. **Route selection from online or social sources** (Instagram, TikTok, app routes, printed Google directions, AI answers) gets people onto terrain they cannot handle. **Phone-only navigation** then fails through battery loss, unsuitable map data or lack of skill. **Delayed or failed alerting** follows (dead phone, no signal, no one knows the plan).
- The share of UK call-outs where people report themselves lost is modest (8% in the 2024 MREW data; about 22% of Scottish mountaineering incidents in 2025 when "lost" and "navigation error" are combined: 107/491, my calculation). Most call-outs are slips and medical events. INFERENCE: navigation aids address one important slice of risk, not most of it, and a product should not imply that navigation features make a trip safe.
- The 2025–2026 cases show two new vectors on top of classic map apps: **AI planning answers** (Shasta) and **GPX files for unofficial routes shared on social media** (Semeru/Candi Jawar; see Q6).

### Gaps
- No national dataset (MREW, SMR, NZMSC, NPS, Basarnas) found that codes "phone or app failure" or "app-sourced route" as an incident factor. The share of incidents caused by phone or app reliance cannot be quantified from public data.
- US: the most recent comprehensive NPS SAR dataset seen was from 2017 (secondary sources), so it was not used. No Parks Canada-specific statement on apps was found.
- The North Shore Rescue article date and the Backpacker follow-up ("two rescues in one week… double-check mapping apps") could not be opened (paywall redirect, 403/429).
- `Influence of digital technologies on hikers' spatial behaviour and perception` (J. Outdoor Recreation & Tourism, 2025) returned 403, so its findings are unknown: [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S221307802500043X)

## Q2. Technical failure modes of phone navigation and offline maps

### Takeaway
Phones fail in the field in predictable ways. The battery dies fastest with the screen on, when the phone keeps searching for a network, and in the cold; iPhones are only specified for 0–35 °C ambient. Position error under forest canopy is typically 5–15 m and varies widely between devices. Map data can be unsuitable (no relief, road-oriented, unofficial routes) or out of date (closures). "Offline" content may not really be stored (cached tiles look downloaded; PWA or browser storage can be evicted). Connectivity-dependent safety features (live sharing, SARLOC, satellite SOS where it is unsupported) quietly stop working exactly when they are needed.

### Cited Findings
**Battery: cold, screen, radio**
- Apple: "iPhone and iPad devices are designed for use where the ambient temperature is between 0º and 35º C" and "Using an iOS or iPadOS device in very cold conditions outside of its operating range might temporarily shorten battery life and could cause your device to turn off." Updated 14 May 2026. VENDOR. [F] — [Apple Support 118431](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118431)
- Mountaineering Scotland: "Batteries run down quicker when cold - keep your phone in an internal pocket close to your body"; "using your touch screen can be difficult or sometimes impossible with thick gloves on". OFFICIAL. [F] — [Mountaineering Scotland](https://www.mountaineering.scot/safety-and-skills/essential-skills/navigation/electronic-navigation-aids)
- Gaia GPS names screen brightness as "one of the biggest drains on your phone's battery". A phone out of coverage is "constantly trying to establish a network connection", so Gaia recommends airplane mode and warns: "you still must bring along a compass and paper map and the skills to use them." 8 Feb 2023 (older). VENDOR. [F] — [Gaia GPS blog](https://blog.gaiagps.com/save-phone-battery-life/)

**Positioning accuracy (GNSS) and the "blue dot"**
- Purfürst (2022, *Sensors*) tested 15 forest sites in Germany. Under canopy, the dual-frequency Xiaomi Mi 10 lite reached DRMS 4.56 m. - Forest plots: RMSE of 4.96–11.45 m with leaves on and 4.51–6.72 m with leaves off, against 1.90–2.36 m in the open. This is attributed to Tomaštík et al., *Forestry* 2017 (older), but was seen only in a search summary. [S] — [Forestry 90(2):187](https://academic.oup.com/forestry/article/90/2/187/2605849)
- Google Maps help: when Maps "isn't sure about your location, a light blue circle shows around the blue dot… You could be anywhere within the light blue circle." [S] — [Google Maps Help 2839911](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/2839911?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid)

[F] — [grough](https://www.grough.co.uk/magazine/2022/08/31/dont-rely-solely-on-what3words-app-say-lakeland-rescuers-sent-to-wrong-side-of-lake). MREW reportedly logged 45 incorrect w3w locations in 12 months, caused by mis-hearing or misspelling (2021, older). [S] — [Slashdot summary](https://tech.slashdot.org/story/21/06/01/1420224/rescuers-question-what3words-use-in-emergencies)

**Physical and handling failures**
- Recommended mitigations: a waterproof case, keeping the phone warm, and "Keeping a simple backup phone in your first aid kit, with a battery that will last for six months". [F] — [BMC](https://www.thebmc.co.uk/en/mobile-phones-in-the-mountains-a-blessing-or-a-curse)
- On Slamet (Dec 2025) one hiker's phone fell and a torch malfunctioned and was later lost, among several items dropped in bad weather. [F] — [Kompas, 16 Jan 2026](https://www.kompas.com/jawa-tengah/read/2026/01/16/134500188/himawan-sampaikan-kronologi-utuh-perpisahan-dengan-syafiq-ali-di?page=all)

### Inferences
- A 5–15 m canopy error, with larger outliers on some phones, is the same order of magnitude as the distance between parallel paths, gullies or a trail and the edge of a ravine. INFERENCE: a blue dot shown without its uncertainty invites the wrong kind of confidence, and off-route logic that ignores accuracy will produce false alarms and false reassurance.
- For a PWA field pack, the Gaia cached-tile warning applies directly. INFERENCE: seeing content while offline does not prove that it is stored durably, because the browser cache or a service-worker runtime cache can later be evicted. Verification should read back from durable storage (IndexedDB or Cache API entries written deliberately), not rely on "it rendered".
- Flight mode saves battery but also blocks incoming SAR contact and SARLOC-style data links. INFERENCE: advice to use flight mode needs a paired rule: after calling for help, keep the phone reachable and stay put (MREW).
- Satellite SOS on iPhone or Pixel should not be presented to Indonesian users as a fallback, because it is not available there as of Jul 2026.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed measurement of smartphone GNSS error in **tropical montane forest or steep volcanic gullies** was verified. The German temperate-forest data is only a proxy. A "Tropical forest tree positioning accuracy" paper appeared in results but was not read.
- No authoritative, method-stated battery-drain figures per hour were found for navigation with the screen on versus off; the only figures are practitioner estimates.
- Evidence on **app crashes** as an incident cause was not found in SAR sources. See file 01 Q7 for user complaints.
- Whether Indonesian 112 and 115 call centres receive handset location automatically (AML/ELS) was not researched.

## Q3. What SAR organisations and outdoor safety bodies recommend about phones, apps and offline maps

### Takeaway
Guidance converges on six points. Treat the phone as one system that can fail, and carry an independent backup. Download proper topographic maps for a wider area before leaving. Protect the battery: full charge, power bank, flight mode, keep it warm. Leave a trip plan with a responsible person, together with an explicit escalation rule. Call the emergency number early and then stay put. Report location in a robust format (grid reference or coordinates plus a verbal description, not an unverified word code). Indonesia's Basarnas adds official registration through posts or official booking apps, licensed guides, and HT radio or a phone with spare batteries, and Indonesia has no phone satellite SOS.

### Cited Findings

[F] — [MREW Getting help](https://www.mountain.rescue.org.uk/getting-help/)

### Inferences
- The recommendations converge on a "two independent systems" principle: phone plus paper map and compass, or phone plus a second device. NZMSC treats a topographic phone app as an acceptable navigation tool, while UK bodies emphasise a paper backup. INFERENCE: MEMOUNT's offline pack fits best as **one of two systems**. A printable version of the same pack would give a low-cost second system.
- Every body places the trip plan and the escalation rule (who calls whom, and when) at the centre. INFERENCE: the value of a trip plan comes from the **contact acting on time**, not from the plan existing. This matters in Indonesia, where Basarnas reports that late reporting and reporting to the wrong agency cost "golden time" (Q6).
- Basarnas lists "peta GPS" and a phone with spare batteries as standard. INFERENCE: Indonesian guidance is not anti-phone. It frames the phone as a coordination and position-update device inside an officially registered, preferably guided, trip.

### Gaps
- No Basarnas document gives specific advice on offline maps, app selection or battery modes; the guidance is general.
- There is no official Indonesian guidance equivalent to the UK advice on grid references or OS Locate for how to state your position (coordinate format, datum, and so on).
- The legality and permit status of satellite messengers (e.g. Garmin inReach/Iridium) in Indonesia was not researched.

## Q4. How responsible apps communicate limits, and what is known about effectiveness

### Takeaway
The better vendor documentation states limits concretely at the point of use: "A data connection is required…", "location will not be updated until you have cellular service again", "airplane mode is not a foolproof way of testing", "clear view of the sky". Some products also surface degraded state to the person watching, such as spotty-connectivity warnings, last known location, battery level and signal status. Evidence that any of these features reduce incidents was **not found**. The only outcome data is a self-reported impact survey (Plan My Walk). Research on warnings suggests generic boilerplate disclaimers are largely ignored and habituate quickly, while specific, contextual and layered messages work better.

### Cited Findings

Contacts see "Safety information, including coordinate info and cell signal strength". The article does not mention contacting emergency services. Updated 3 Sep 2026. **Evidence on effectiveness**
- Plan My Walk impact surveys (2022 and 2024, with Rangahau Aotearoa; sample size not published) found: "84% agree that Plan My Walk makes planning and preparation easier"; 87% say it "combines essential info"; "83% of users expressed improved confidence"; "42% of users have changed or modified their plans based on information they found in Plan My Walk". These are self-reported, with no incident-outcome measure; published Dec 2024. [F] — [NZMSC: Measuring the impact of Plan My Walk](https://www.mountainsafety.org.nz/explore/research-and-insights/measuring-the-impact-of-plan-my-walk)
- No study or dataset was found showing that live-sharing (Beacon, Live Share), wrong-turn alerts, offline-ready checks or in-app disclaimers reduce SAR incidents, search time or fatalities.

**Evidence on disclaimers and warnings (PEER-REVIEWED)**
- Obar & Oeldorf-Hirsch (Information, Communication & Society, 2020; N = 543): 74% skipped the privacy policy through a "quick join" clickwrap. The terms of service were read for an average of 51 seconds against 15–17 minutes needed, and 97% and 93% agreed. [S] — [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/1369118X.2018.1486870)
- Anderson, Vance et al. (fMRI, 2016, with a longitudinal follow-up in MIS Quarterly 2018): neural response to warnings drops sharply after the **second** exposure. This is framed as biology, not laziness. "Polymorphic" warnings, whose appearance varies, reduced habituation. [S] — [Frontiers/PMC7751389](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7751389/); [MISQ 2018](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.25300/MISQ/2018/14124); [BYU: polymorphic warnings](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/facpub/9306/)
- Wogalter's C-HIP model: a warning must win attention, be understood, fit the person's beliefs and motivate before it changes behaviour, and failure at any stage is a bottleneck. Specific statements work better than general ones. [S] — [C-HIP chapter PDF](https://www.safetyhumanfactors.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/281%29Wogalter%282006%29%28d%29Chap5.pdf); [Research-Based Guidelines for Warning Design](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/11221385_Research-Based_Guidelines_for_Warning_Design_and_Evaluation)
- Engeset et al. (NHESS 2018, Norwegian avalanche warnings; 110 experts, 264 users): "a simple danger level is not enough to convey the intended warning message on lower danger levels". Comprehension depended on competence at level 2 (p = 0.006) but not at level 4. The "avalanche problem" was rated the most important element by 67% of users and 77% of experts. Recommendations include "Less is more". (Older, but the canonical mountain-warning comprehension study.) [F] — [NHESS 18:2537](https://nhess.copernicus.org/articles/18/2537/2018/)

### Inferences
- The strongest vendor pattern (INFERENCE) is **stating degraded state as data**, not blanket disclaimers. Examples: last-update time, "spotty connectivity", battery and signal shown to contacts, faded icons for incomplete downloads.
- Safety copy placed only at sign-up or in terms will very likely be skipped (Obar). Repeated identical banners stop being seen after one or two exposures (Anderson). INFERENCE: MEMOUNT safety messages should be few and tied to a moment (download, start of hike mode, low battery, poor accuracy, off-route, overdue). They should be specific to the trail and the action ("turn back by 10:00 at Pos 5") and layered: a short headline, the specific problem, then the action.
- Engeset's "danger level alone is insufficient at moderate levels" maps onto trail labels. INFERENCE: a Route Fit label or grade alone (COCOK / PERLU PERSIAPAN / KURANG COCOK; Kemenhut Grade I–V) under-communicates the concrete problem at moderate levels. The UI should name the problem, for example "jalur berpasir lepas di atas Kalimati; kabut sore hari", in line with R-003 and R-005.

### Gaps
- No evaluation of *hiking-app* safety messaging (A/B tests, field studies) was found in public sources.
- Plan My Walk's published impact figures lack sample size and method details.
- It is unknown whether AllTrails, Strava or Komoot publish any safety-outcome data; none was found.

## Q5. Human factors: over-reliance, blue-dot confidence, cognition under cold and fatigue, and how risk is communicated

### Takeaway
Automated positioning reliably reduces how much the navigator learns about the environment. People following an automated system often cannot retrace the route without it, and heavy lifetime GPS use is associated with poorer spatial memory. Cold impairs attention, processing speed, executive function and memory **before** hypothermia sets in. Verbal risk terms are interpreted very differently from person to person, while numeric **ranges and uncertainty** improve decisions and trust. That supports showing uncertainty for position and time estimates, not safety probabilities.

### Cited Findings
- Brügger, Richter & Fabrikant (Cognitive Research: Principles & Implications, 2019; N = 64, real outdoor route): performance while following the route did not differ by system behaviour. However, "participants using systems with higher levels of automation seemed not to acquire enough spatial knowledge to reverse the route without navigation errors." Leaving self-localisation or attention allocation to the navigator increased spatial learning "without harming navigation performance". PEER-REVIEWED. [F, abstract via Europe PMC] — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41235-019-0156-5)
- Dahmani & Bohbot (Scientific Reports, 2020; 50 drivers, 13 retested after 3 years): "people with greater lifetime GPS experience have worse spatial memory during self-guided navigation". Greater GPS use between sessions was associated with "a steeper decline in hippocampal-dependent spatial memory". It is a small longitudinal sample, as the authors note. [F, abstract via Europe PMC] — [Nature Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-62877-0)
- Ishikawa et al. (J. Environmental Psychology, 2008, older): GPS-assisted walkers made more errors and acquired poorer survey knowledge than users of paper maps or of direct experience. [S] — [ResearchGate](https://researchgate.net/publication/223739400_Wayfinding_with_a_GPS-based_mobile_navigation_system_A_comparison_with_maps_and_direct_experience)
- There is 2025 work on landmark-based instructions to limit GPS dependence; only the title was seen. [S] — [J. Environmental Psychology 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0272494425002968)
- Falla, Micarelli, Hüfner & Strapazzon (IJERPH, 2021; systematic review of 18 studies): "Cold exposure induced in most of the experimental settings (15 of 18) an impairment of CP even before accidental hypothermia was established. The most investigated and affected cognitive domains were attention and processing speed, executive function, and memory." PEER-REVIEWED. [F, abstract via Europe PMC] — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/18/18/9725)
- A randomised cross-over study (Scientific Reports, 2026) found that 15 minutes of cold exposure slowed reactions, increased lapses and reduced risky choices. [S] — [Nature Sci Rep 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-38048-y)
- Joslyn & LeClerc (J. Experimental Psychology: Applied, 2012, older): adding numeric uncertainty to weather forecasts improved precautionary decisions and trust compared with single-value forecasts. Their 2013 review adds that the benefit holds across education levels. [S] — [APA PDF 2012](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/features/xap-18-1-126.pdf); [Current Directions in Psych. Science 2013](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0963721413481473)
- Verbal probability terms vary widely in interpretation. In one surgical-risk survey the same word, "common", was read as 45.6% for one complication and 61.7% for another, and variance was higher for verbal terms than for numbers or ranges. [S] — [PubMed 35129504](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35129504/); [systematic review PMC11067312](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11067312/)
- Usage prevalence: Mountaineering Scotland's 2022 survey found 87% had navigated with a phone or GPS [F]. Among 560 Pacific Crest Trail thru-hikers, about 97% carried smartphones [S] — [Annals of Leisure Research](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/11745398.2021.1993286). A German study found digital tools used for planning (86%) and navigating (73%). [S] — [German J. Exercise & Sport Research 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12662-023-00927-1)
- Social-media pull: MREW cites "Instagram posts" (2025) [F]. Basarnas head Marsdya Mohammad Syafii (16 Jul 2026): "Jangan pernah mendaki hanya demi konten" and warns against "keberanian yang tidak disertai dengan kesiapan matang". [F] — [Kompas](https://megapolitan.kompas.com/read/2026/07/16/16201331/jangan-pernah-mendaki-hanya-demi-konten-ini-tips-aman-naik-gunung-agar)
- AI over-reliance: the Shasta case (Q1). Gemini supplied an 8-hour estimate and packing advice that the group followed. [F] — [KRCR](https://krcrtv.com/news/local/sheriff-discusses-mount-shasta-rescue-after-hikers-relied-on-ai-to-plan-trip)

### Inferences
- "Blue-dot overconfidence" is not a formal construct in the papers found. The mechanism is documented, though: automation reduces attention to the environment and lowers the spatial knowledge available when the device fails (Brügger; Dahmani & Bohbot). INFERENCE: the risk is highest exactly when the phone dies mid-route, the failure mode in the NH and Semeru anecdotes.
- Brügger's finding suggests a design lever (INFERENCE/HYPOTHESIS): let the user trigger self-localisation ("Cek posisi") and ask them to confirm arrival at named checkpoints, instead of continuous auto-follow. This may preserve orientation skills and saves battery. It needs field testing before any claim is made.
- Cold and fatigue degrade attention before hypothermia (Falla). INFERENCE: hike-mode UI must work for an impaired user: very few elements, large targets, no multi-step flows for emergency actions, and emergency info reachable in one tap from the lock state.
- Risk communication (INFERENCE consistent with R-005): numeric uncertainty is appropriate for **measurements and estimates** (position ±m, time ranges such as "2–3 jam", data age "4 jam lalu"). It is not appropriate for **safety or fit probabilities**, which R-005 forbids. Verbal labels (COCOK, and so on) need the concrete "problem + action" text next to them (Engeset).

### Gaps
- No study was found of phone-navigation over-reliance among **Indonesian** hikers, or of hypoxia and cognition at Indonesian summit altitudes (about 3,000–3,700 m). A hypoxia meta-analysis appeared in results but was not read.
- No HCI study was found testing accuracy-circle comprehension among hikers.

## Q6. Indonesia-specific: incident patterns, Basarnas guidance, Balai rules, and any official stance on apps

### Takeaway
Public Indonesian evidence points to these incident drivers: getting lost in fog or rain (often at dusk or on descent), illegal or non-official routes (with no HM markers and outside SAR planning, sometimes coordinated on TikTok and navigated by GPX on a phone), hypothermia at cold high camps and summits, separation from the group, and late or misdirected alerting. The state's 2025–2026 response is structural rather than app-based. It includes Kemenhut's national trail grading (81 routes, Grade I–V), Rinjani's SOP (proof of experience, guide ratios, premium insurance, a health certificate dated one day before), Semeru's RFID checkpoint bracelets, nationwide blacklists for illegal climbers, and Basarnas' SOP centred on official registration. No official Indonesian statement evaluating hiking **navigation** apps was found.

### Inferences
- In the Indonesian cases, the alarm is typically raised by a **phone call or WhatsApp from the lost person to family or group members, or by family noticing an overdue return**, and then relayed to Basarnas (Gede, Batukaru, Semeru). INFERENCE: a trip plan held by a named contact who has explicit instructions to call 115 or 112 directly addresses the "late or misdirected report" factor that Basarnas names.
- Illegal routes combine every failure mode: no markers, not in registration, possibly closed for volcanic hazard, and navigated with a GPX file on a phone. INFERENCE: an offline pack must never show or accept non-official geometry as a route. This is consistent with R-022 (publication gate) and R-009/R-010 (community does not override official).
- Grading and SOPs (grade, guide requirement, turnaround or "10:00" rules, health certificates) are official, trail-level data. INFERENCE: they belong in the pack as authority-labelled facts with a source and date. Where they are unknown, they should be shown as UNKNOWN (R-006, R-007), not omitted in a way that implies "no requirement".

### Gaps
- No national, **hiker-specific** SAR statistics were found; the Basarnas KMM category mixes hikers with drownings and other emergencies. Per-office breakdowns of mountain operations were not found.
- No official Indonesian statement (Basarnas, Kemenhut, Balai) was found on the reliability of hiking **navigation** apps, community GPX sharing, or removing illegal routes from apps and maps.
- No data was found on how many Indonesian SAR cases involved phone battery failure or reliance on offline maps.
- Whether 112 is live in each kabupaten covering major mountains (Lumajang, Malang, Lombok Timur, and others) was not verified.

## Q7. Design implications for MEMOUNT (objective 3): copy, defaults and features

### Takeaway
The evidence supports designing the offline field pack and hike mode as an **honest second system**. It should show what it knows and how fresh and how precise that knowledge is: data age, verification state, position uncertainty, battery and connectivity. It should push users into the protective loop around it: official registration, a printed or independent backup, and a trip plan held by a person with an explicit escalation rule to 115 or 112. It should never imply safety, monitoring or rescue capability it does not have. No evidence shows that any in-app safety feature reduces incidents, so every mechanism below is a design hypothesis to test (per `docs/PROTOKOL-UJI-KEGUNAAN.md`), not a proven control.

### Cited Findings
Each evidence item below is followed by the design lever it supports.
- A phone is "not always the most reliable way of calling for help" (MREW); "Don't depend solely on technology" (AdventureSmart); "No system is 100% fail safe" (Mountaineering Scotland). This supports framing the pack as one of two systems. [F] — [MREW](https://www.mountain.rescue.org.uk/getting-help/); [AdventureSmart](https://www.adventuresmart.ca/trip-plan/); [Mountaineering Scotland](https://www.mountaineering.scot/safety-and-skills/essential-skills/navigation/electronic-navigation-aids)
- Cached or incomplete content can look offline-ready, and an airplane-mode test is "not a foolproof way of testing" (Gaia). PWA storage is evicted by LRU under pressure, and persistence is granted heuristically (WebKit). This supports verifying pack contents from durable storage and re-checking them. [API] — [Gaia](https://help.gaiagps.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003523707-Check-if-a-Map-is-Downloaded-and-Available-Offline-on-the-iOS-app); [F] — [WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/14403/updates-to-storage-policy/)
- Under canopy, error is 4.6–14.6 m depending on the phone (Purfürst), and Google shows an uncertainty circle. This supports displaying accuracy and fix age, and making off-route logic accuracy-aware. [F] — [PMC8838512](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8838512/); [S] — [Google Maps Help](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/2839911?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid)
- Battery is lost to cold (outside 0–35 °C), screen use and network searching, and phones died mid-navigation in the NH and Semeru cases. This supports low-power defaults, reserve thresholds and keep-warm advice. [F] — [Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118431); [Gaia blog](https://blog.gaiagps.com/save-phone-battery-life/); [Fox Weather](https://www.foxweather.com/lifestyle/hiker-rescued-white-mountains-new-hampshire-may-2025)
- Trip plans need content plus an escalation protocol (NZMSC: wait one hour, then call 111), and "If no one knows you are missing, no one will be looking for you." Basarnas notes that late or misdirected reports cost golden time. This supports a plan share with explicit contact instructions naming 115 or 112. [F] — [NZMSC](https://www.mountainsafety.org.nz/learn/skills/leaving-trip-intentions); [AdventureSmart](https://www.adventuresmart.ca/trip-plan/); [PDF] — [Basarnas LAKIP 2024](https://basarnas.go.id/storage/files/9/LAKIP%202024/10.%20Laporan%20Kinerja%20Direkrorat%20Operasi%202024.pdf)
- Live-sharing products need data and say so; the better ones show last-known location, battery and signal to contacts (AllTrails, Strava). Plan My Walk does not track at all. This supports never implying monitoring without live data, and showing freshness when data is shown. [API]/[F] — [AllTrails](https://support.alltrails.com/hc/en-us/articles/37212858771348-How-to-use-Live-Share); [Strava](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401829-strava-beacon); [PMW FAQ](https://www.mountainsafety.org.nz/plan-my-walk-frequently-asked-questions)
- Location reporting fails with word codes (w3w), while grid references or coordinates plus a verbal description work. This supports an offline emergency card with coordinates, the nearest checkpoint and elevation. [F] — [grough](https://www.grough.co.uk/magazine/2022/08/31/dont-rely-solely-on-what3words-app-say-lakeland-rescuers-sent-to-wrong-side-of-lake)
- "Stop, assess the situation, and seek assistance early"; do not self-correct into unfamiliar terrain (NZ coroner). "Do NOT change your position until contacted" (MREW). This supports the wording of off-route and lost prompts. [F] — [NZMSC](https://www.mountainsafety.org.nz/discover/read/coroners-findings-highlight-importance-of-navigation-tools); [MREW](https://www.mountain.rescue.org.uk/getting-help/)
- Boilerplate is skipped (Obar), repetition habituates (Anderson), specific beats general (Wogalter), and a level alone is insufficient (Engeset). This supports few, contextual, specific and layered messages. [S]/[F] — see Q4 sources
- Higher automation lowers spatial learning without improving route-following (Brügger), and cold impairs cognition before hypothermia (Falla). This supports user-initiated position checks, checkpoint confirmation and a minimal hike-mode UI. [F] — [Brügger 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41235-019-0156-5); [Falla 2021](https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/18/18/9725)
- Unofficial and illegal routes, closures and AI planning errors drive incidents (NSR, Semeru, Merbabu, Shasta). This supports official-only geometry, dated official status, and no AI-authored routes or logistics. [S]/[F] — see Q1 and Q6

### Inferences
Labels follow R-023. Anything that changes product scope, privacy or data authority needs owner approval under R-021 and `docs/KEBIJAKAN-DATA-PRIBADI.md`.

**A. Copy (RECOMMENDATION)**
1. Frame the pack at download and at hike-mode start in one line. Draft: "Paket luring membantu kamu mengikuti jalur resmi. Bawa cadangan: peta cetak atau perangkat lain." Do not use "aman", "terjamin" or "pasti" anywhere (R-005, R-012, R-014). Keep the sentence short and specific, and do not repeat it on every screen (habituation).
2. Show official status as a dated fact that is explicitly not a safety claim. Draft: "Status resmi: DIBUKA · sumber: [Balai/BB-TN] · diperbarui [tanggal jam]. Dibuka bukan berarti aman." If the status is older than a freshness threshold, show "Status belum diperbarui sejak [tanggal]; cek di pos registrasi" instead of the old value (R-012, R-013). The threshold is a product decision (OPEN ISSUE).
3. Positioning copy states uncertainty numerically. Draft: "Posisi ±25 m · 2 menit lalu". When accuracy is poor: "Sinyal GPS lemah; posisi belum pasti. Jangan tinggalkan jalur untuk mengejar titik." This is numeric uncertainty about a measurement, not a safety percentage, so it is consistent with R-005.
4. Off-route or lost prompts use SAR wording. Draft: "Kamu tampak di luar jalur resmi (±[jarak] m). Berhenti. Kembali ke checkpoint terakhir yang kamu kenali. Jangan memotong lereng." For the "tersesat" path: "Berhenti di tempat aman, hubungi 112/115, lalu tetap di tempat sampai dihubungi tim SAR."
5. Estimates are ranges from official or user-own pace, never competitive. Draft: "Perkiraan ke [Pos 5]: 1–1,5 jam (dari catatan pace kamu sendiri)". Show any official cut-off ("Batas puncak: 10.00 menurut pengelola") as a sourced fact, and show UNKNOWN where none is known (R-006, R-007, R-015). Evidence: underestimating travel time is a major SAR factor (NZMSC 30%), and the Slamet group summited at 14:00 against a 10:00 rule.

**B. Defaults (RECOMMENDATION / HYPOTHESIS)**
6. Hike mode defaults to low power: dark UI, screen off between checks, no continuous map animation, and position fixes on demand or at a modest interval (HYPOTHESIS: to be measured on real mid-range Android devices common in Indonesia). Offer flight-mode guidance paired with the rule "setelah menelepon darurat, matikan mode pesawat dan tetap bisa dihubungi".
7. Battery reserve thresholds, for example announcing at 50%, 30% and 15% (HYPOTHESIS: the values are a design choice, not evidence-based). At the reserve level, switch to an emergency-only view (emergency card plus last known coordinates). Remind users to keep the phone in an inner pocket at cold camps (Apple 0–35 °C; high camps below 5 °C).
8. Off-route alerts are **accuracy-aware**. Suppress or soften the alert when the accuracy radius is larger than the off-route threshold. AllTrails uses a fixed 50 m with no accuracy caveat. MEMOUNT's threshold should be tested against canopy error (HYPOTHESIS). Use no alarm sounds that could encourage hurried corrective moves.

**C. Features (RECOMMENDATION; items marked (approval) need owner sign-off)**
9. **"Siap luring" pack verification.** Each component (official geometry, checkpoints, elevation profile, trip plan, preparation list, emergency info) gets a stored/verified state, size and version date, read back from durable storage rather than inferred from rendering. Re-verify automatically when hike mode opens and show "Terakhir diverifikasi: [tanggal jam]". Request persistent storage, and recommend installing to the home screen with the reason stated (WebKit heuristics; Safari eviction). A pack that fails verification says so plainly and blocks the "siap" state (R-007: missing is not "ok").
10. **Last-synced indicators everywhere dynamic data appears.** Official status, closures, weather (source, time and reference area; R-013) and community condition notes (labelled COMMUNITY, below OFFICIAL; R-009, R-010) each show their age. Community notes never override official status offline.
11. **Printable pack** (PDF or print view: route sketch, checkpoint list with descriptions and elevations, turnaround times, emergency card, trip plan). This provides the paper second system that UK bodies ask for, at near-zero cost. Plan My Walk reportedly offers a PDF of the plan. [S]
12. **Trip plan shared with a contact.** The contents follow NZMSC/AdventureSmart: official trail name, registration or booking number, group members, start time, expected return, turnaround time, alternative routes, equipment and expected coverage. Send it as plain text through WhatsApp or SMS so it works on low bandwidth and needs no account (INFERENCE from the Indonesian cases, where alerts travel through WhatsApp and family). Put the instructions to the contact **in the message itself**. Draft: "Kalau belum ada kabar sampai [jam kembali + 1 jam]: hubungi basecamp [nomor jika resmi diketahui], lalu Basarnas 115 atau 112. Sebutkan jalur, nomor registrasi, jumlah anggota, checkpoint terakhir yang dikabarkan." The one-hour buffer copies NZMSC practice; local validation with Basarnas or a Balai is an OPEN ISSUE. State clearly that **MEMOUNT does not monitor the trip** unless a live feature exists.
13. **Live location sharing** is not currently in scope. It is a new feature with location-privacy implications and needs approval (R-021; KEBIJAKAN-DATA-PRIBADI). If it is ever added, copy the honest patterns: "data diperlukan", last update time, "sinyal putus-putus" warnings to contacts, battery and signal state (AllTrails, Strava).
14. **Offline emergency card, reachable in one tap from hike mode, with no login or network needed.** It shows 112 (general, free) and 115 (Basarnas) as tap-to-call; the basecamp or Balai contact only if officially known, otherwise "tidak diketahui"; "tetap di tempat setelah menelepon"; and a location block to read aloud: decimal lat/long, nearest checkpoint name, elevation, trail name, and the time of the fix. Add a "Salin/Kirim lokasi via SMS/WA" action that composes the text for when data is weak but SMS works (HYPOTHESIS). Use no word-code location systems (w3w evidence). Do not mention phone satellite SOS, which is unavailable in Indonesia.
15. **Registration and legality gate in the pack.** Link to the official booking channel. Show the Kemenhut grade and the trail's official requirements (guide, insurance, health certificate) with source and date, or UNKNOWN. Show only official, published trails (R-022). Import or display of community GPX as a "route" should not exist in the pack (INFERENCE from the Semeru and Merbabu illegal-route cases). Any such capability would be a product change that needs approval.
16. **Anti-over-reliance mechanics** (HYPOTHESIS to test in the field). Position checks are user-initiated ("Cek posisi"). The next checkpoint is shown with a landmark description, distance and elevation gain, not just an arrow. The user confirms arrival at each checkpoint ("Sudah di Pos 3?"). The trip plan records the last confirmed checkpoint for the contact message. Rationale: Brügger, Dahmani & Bohbot, and battery saving.
17. **No AI-authored routes, time estimates or packing lists presented as authoritative** (R-011; Shasta case). If AI summarisation exists elsewhere in MEMOUNT, it must be labelled and must not appear in the field pack.
18. **Readiness wording stays decision support** (R-014). A checklist completion count such as "9 dari 12 item" is fine, but not "Kamu siap/aman". Pre-departure prompts are specific and few: "Isi daya 100% + power bank", "Paket terverifikasi", "Rencana terkirim ke [nama]", "Registrasi: [nomor]", "Cadangan cetak dibawa?"

### Gaps
- There is **no outcome evidence** that any of the recommended mechanisms (verification checks, accuracy display, trip-plan messages, battery thresholds, checkpoint confirmation) reduce incidents or search time. They need usability and field testing with Indonesian hikers on real devices, including mid-range Android phones, in cold and wet conditions.
- Local escalation norms (who the contact should call first: basecamp, Balai or Basarnas; how long to wait) need validation with Basarnas or Balai staff. The NZ one-hour rule is only a reference.
- Accuracy thresholds for tropical montane forest and volcanic gullies are unknown (Q2 gap), so off-route parameters cannot yet be set from evidence.
- Whether Indonesian 112 and 115 receive automatic handset location (AML/ELS) is unknown. The emergency card should assume they do not, and support verbal read-out.
